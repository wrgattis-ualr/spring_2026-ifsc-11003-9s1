About Arkansas Children's Hospital

Arkansas Children's Hospital (ACH) is a pediatric hospital and is the only Level I trauma center for pediatric patients in the state of Arkansas. It is among the largest in the United States, serving infants, children, teens, and young adults from birth to age 21. The hospital also features the only level 4 NICU in the state, which means they can care for newborns with the most complex health requirements. ACH is affiliated with the University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences and is a teaching hospital with the UAMS College of Medicine's Department of Pediatrics. It is also a record setting hospital. In 2011, Arkansas Children's Hospital set a national record for pediatric heart transplants conducted in a year. The hospital transplanted new hearts into 31 children and adults that year.

It has been featured in several publications and media outlets for its innovative care and achievements in pediatric medicine.
ACH has been profiled a number of times on the Discovery Health Channel. In the fall of 2007, the program "Surgery Saved My Life" examined how three physicians in the Arkansas Children's Hospital Vascular Anomalies Center of Excellence handled patients' potentially fatal vascular tumors. Featured in the documentary were James Suen, M.D., a head and neck surgeon at ACH and the University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences (UAMS); Lisa Buckmiller, M.D., medical director of the ACH Vascular Anomalies Center; and Rick Jackson, M.D., a surgeon at the hospital and associate professor of surgery in the UAMS College of Medicine. One patient featured in the profile was a 12-year-old girl who was born in Shanghai with what is believed to be the largest vascular tumor ever seen; Chinese surgeons had been reluctant to operate on the tumor.
Level I Trauma center
A Level 1 trauma center is the highest-level, comprehensive regional resource hospital verified by the American College of Surgeons (ACS) or state authority, capable of providing total 24/7 care for every aspect of injury, from prevention through rehabilitation. These centers manage the most complex, life-threatening cases with specialized, in-house surgical teams.
Level IV NICU
A Level IV NICU provides the highest, most specialized care for the most critically ill, extremely premature, or low-birth-weight newborns.
