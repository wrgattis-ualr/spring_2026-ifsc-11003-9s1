A Legacy of Discovery

NASA's "Follow the Water" strategy has guided decades of Martian exploration. What began as a quest to find evidence of past water has evolved into a sophisticated search for the building blocks of life itself.

Curiosity

Key Mission Timeline

  • Nov 26, 2011: Launch from Cape Canaveral, Florida.
  • Aug 6, 2012: Touchdown in Gale Crater, Mars.
  • 2013: Confirmed ancient freshwater lake existence, providing key evidence of habitable conditions.
  • Sept 2014: Reached Mount Sharp, a mountain of layered rock providing insights into geological time.
  • 2016-2017: Detected complex organic molecules within sedimentary rocks.
  • 2014-Present: Ongoing operation, with multiple mission extensions to investigate climate and geological history.

Key Discoveries & Capabilities

  • Habitability: Confirmed Mars once had environmental conditions (water, nutrients) suitable for supporting microbial life.
  • Organics: Discovered complex organic molecules—the carbon-based building blocks of life—within rock samples.
  • Mount Sharp: Analyzed lower layers of the mountain to understand the transition from a wet, ancient Mars to the cold, dry desert of today.

Perseverance

Key Mission Timeline

  • July 30, 2020: Launch from Cape Canaveral, Florida.
  • Feb 18, 2021: Touchdown in Jezero Crater, an ancient river delta.
  • April 19, 2021: The Ingenuity helicopter achieved the first powered, controlled flight on another planet.
  • Sept 1, 2021: Successfully collected the first core sample of Martian rock.
  • 2022-Present: Exploring the "Delta Front" to search for preserved signs of ancient microbial life.

Key Discoveries & Capabilities

  • Astrobiology: Specifically designed to "Seek Signs of Life" by searching for biosignatures in ancient river sediments.
  • MOXIE: Successfully generated breathable oxygen from the thin Martian atmosphere—a critical test for future human explorers.
  • Sample Caching: Collected and sealed the first-ever rock samples intended for return to Earth by a future joint mission.
  • Ingenuity Helicopter: Proved that aerial scouting is possible on Mars, flying over 70 times before its mission concluded.
