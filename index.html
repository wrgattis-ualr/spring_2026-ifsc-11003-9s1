Thoughts on Progressive Enhancement

Progressive enhancement is a method of inclusive web design that's based on bottom-up design as opposed to the older top-down design of graceful degradation. Starting from the bottom-up with HTML ensures that the content of the webpage is functional on all devices, regardles of age.

Progressive enhancmenet is important in modern web development due to the plethora of devices capable of accessing the web and the increase in internet users around the world due to improving infastructure. Improving accessibility by focusing on the baseline content allows your website to reach a larger audience.

Mars Rover Project Revision and Reflection

I was overwhelmed at the beginning of the semester, but I began to enjoy it more as we got deeper into the course. I took this class with Professor Wallace as the instructor over 15 years ago and got a C. I felt completely lost in 2011, but I really felt like I understood most of the concepts as I began working on the final project this go-around. I have ADHD so I struggle to keep up with lectures in real-time. Having Professor Wallace walk us through the HTML Prototype, CSS Demo, Image manipulation, and the JS really aided me in this course compared to 15 years ago. Having those projects and being able to go back and review them and the lecture recordings as a resource when I got stuck was invaluable for me.

I also really enjoyed working in Codepen and seeing my website develop from a skeleton html document to a more interactive webpage with CSS and JS. If I had to start over, I would focus more on CSS. Even through the final project, I was still occasionally going back to our older projects/lectures to review development concepts that I was stuck on, so more practice would have been better for me in hindsight.

I was fortunate with feedback. I am my grandmother's primary caregiver, so she lives with me. She has macular degeneration, which means she's losing her central eyesight and only has peripheral vision. She's dependent on screen readers to navigate and having her review my website reinforced my focus on accessibility for people dependent on screen readers. I was able to integrate the visually-hidden class, aria-current, and aria-label to improve the ability of screen readers to navigate through a website. Overall, I really enjoyed this course and wish I had more time to devote to it this semester. I'm taking 18 hours, have two elementary school kids, and have a full time job, but I would definitely like to learn more about web development when my life slows down and I finish my nursing degree.
